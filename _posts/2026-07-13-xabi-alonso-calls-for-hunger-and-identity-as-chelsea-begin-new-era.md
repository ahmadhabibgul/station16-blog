Chelsea manager Xabi Alonso has called on his players to show greater hunger, passion and determination as he prepares for his first Premier League season in charge of the club.

The Spaniard takes over a Chelsea side looking for a fresh start after a disappointing 2025-26 campaign, where the club finished 10th in the Premier League, failed to win a trophy and missed out on European football.

Speaking at his first press conference as Chelsea boss, Alonso highlighted the importance of building a strong mentality and improving the small details that can decide matches in one of the world’s most competitive leagues.

> “We need hunger, passion, we need to want to improve,” Alonso said. “The Premier League is so competitive. We need to prepare ourselves, we need to be determined in the details, in how we want to play.”

## Squad Changes Underway

Alonso also addressed ongoing uncertainty surrounding several players as Chelsea continue to reshape their squad ahead of the new season.

Winger Alejandro Garnacho, who joined from Manchester United but struggled for consistency last season, has attracted interest from other clubs. The Argentine scored just once in 24 Premier League appearances after his move to Stamford Bridge.

“There is interest in him from other clubs, so let's see how this develops,” Alonso said. “Hopefully it finishes in the best possible way for all parties.”

The Chelsea manager was also asked about the future of midfielder Enzo Fernandez. While he avoided discussing long-term plans in detail, Alonso confirmed he had spoken with the Argentina international and hopes he remains part of his project.

Chelsea have so far made limited moves in the transfer market, with their only major arrival being right-back Marco Palestra from Serie A side Atalanta on a contract running until 2033.

Alonso praised the young defender’s versatility and mentality, saying his ability to play in multiple defensive positions fits the demands of modern football.

## Building a New Chelsea Identity

Chelsea’s absence from European competition next season will reduce their schedule, but Alonso insisted it would not change his overall approach.

“The main focus for how we want to start in August does not change much,” he said. “What we need to reinforce is clear, but we are working on it.”

The appointment marks Alonso’s return to English football, where he previously enjoyed a successful playing career with Liverpool before moving to Real Madrid and later Bayern Munich.

After beginning his coaching career in Real Madrid’s academy, Alonso gained widespread recognition during his time at Bayer Leverkusen, where he developed an attacking style and established himself as one of Europe’s most promising young managers.

At Chelsea, he inherits a club searching for stability after a turbulent period that saw two managers leave during the previous season. Alonso’s priority will be creating a clear playing style and bringing the squad together.

“To be part of that process, how we want to play, how we want to approach a game, that's my goal,” Alonso said.

Chelsea will begin their Premier League campaign on August 24 against Fulham as Alonso looks to start a new chapter at Stamford Bridge.
