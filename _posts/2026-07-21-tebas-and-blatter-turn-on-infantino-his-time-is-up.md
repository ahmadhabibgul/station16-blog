Gianni Infantino's grip on FIFA is facing its loudest public challenge in years, as La Liga president Javier Tebas and former FIFA president Sepp Blatter both call for him to step aside in the wake of a contentious 2026 World Cup.

The tournament wrapped up on Sunday with Spain edging a 10-man Argentina 1-0 in the final at MetLife Stadium, Ferran Torres scoring the winner after Enzo Fernández's red card. But even as Spain celebrated, criticism of the man running the sport's global governing body was mounting.

## Tebas: "I Think His Time Is Up"

Speaking to Italian outlet *La Gazzetta dello Sport*, Tebas didn't mince words when asked whether Infantino should resign: "In my opinion, yes, I think his time is up."

The La Liga chief pointed to a string of grievances from the tournament, chief among them the handling of USMNT forward Folarin Balogun's suspension. After a personal phone call from Donald Trump to Infantino, FIFA agreed to lift the ban — a decision Tebas called "an extremely serious matter," adding that FIFA was fortunate Belgium eliminated the U.S. before the situation escalated further.

Tebas also took aim at FIFA's growing appetite for expansion, dismissing talk of a 64-team World Cup as nonsensical, and mocked the introduction of hydration breaks in stadiums that were already air-conditioned, accusing FIFA of running the sport "for its own interests, certainly not for football."

Still, Tebas was clear-eyed about the odds of change. He described FIFA's electoral system as "rotten," noting there's no credible opposition candidate willing to challenge Infantino. "He shouldn't stay," Tebas said, "but the current state of affairs means he won't leave."

## Blatter Joins the Chorus

In an unexpected twist, Infantino's own predecessor piled on. Sepp Blatter — who resigned from FIFA in 2015 amid a sprawling corruption scandal and remains banned from the sport — said the World Cup had "lost its credibility" under Infantino's leadership. He called on "fans, players and national associations to reclaim football and bring it back to its roots under new leadership," and warned that politics must not be allowed to overshadow the game, an apparent reference to Infantino's close relationship with Trump.

## A Wider Rift

The criticism from Tebas and Blatter comes amid broader tension between FIFA and other power centers in the sport. UEFA president Aleksander Ceferin skipped the World Cup final entirely, a reflection of strained relations between the two organizations over disciplinary decisions, refereeing, and match operations during the tournament.

## What Happens Next

Despite the backlash, Infantino shows no signs of stepping back. He is expected to seek a fourth term as FIFA president, with the vote for the 2027–2031 cycle scheduled for March 18 in Morocco — and, barring a surprise challenger, he is likely to run unopposed.

Whether the criticism from Tebas and Blatter amounts to more than noise remains to be seen. Both men bring credibility questions of their own — Tebas has sparred with Infantino for years, and Blatter's own tenure ended in disgrace — but their aligned message adds to a growing sense that unease with Infantino's leadership extends well beyond his usual critics.
