England's hopes of reaching their first men's World Cup final since 1966 were shattered in heartbreaking fashion after Argentina scored twice in the closing stages to secure a dramatic 2-1 victory in Atlanta.

Anthony Gordon's second-half strike had put the Three Lions within touching distance of Sunday's final against Spain, but late goals from Enzo Fernandez and Lautaro Martinez completed a stunning turnaround for the reigning world champions, with Lionel Messi providing assists for both goals.

The defeat has sparked widespread criticism of England manager Thomas Tuchel, whose decision to abandon an attacking approach in favour of a defensive setup has come under intense scrutiny.

After Gordon's opener in the 55th minute, England gradually retreated deeper into their own half, allowing Argentina to dominate possession and dictate the tempo. Tuchel reinforced his defence by introducing Ezri Konsa before later sending on Dan Burn and Nico O'Reilly, switching to a back five in an attempt to preserve the lead.

The tactical change had the opposite effect.

Argentina piled on sustained pressure throughout the final stages before Fernandez levelled the score in the 85th minute with a powerful finish. Deep into stoppage time, Martinez headed home Messi's cross to complete the comeback and send Argentina into the final.

England legends were quick to question Tuchel's approach.

Former captain Wayne Rooney argued that the substitutions changed the mentality of the team, suggesting the players lost belief after seeing defensive reinforcements introduced instead of attacking options.

"The decisions Thomas Tuchel has made have cost us tonight," Rooney said. "You can't go a goal up and then surrender the ball against players of that quality."

Gary Lineker also criticised the tactical shift, describing the decision to sit deep against a side led by Messi as "astonishing" and saying England's defensive approach played directly into Argentina's strengths.

Former goalkeeper Joe Hart drew comparisons to Gareth Southgate's tenure, noting that England appeared to repeat the same pattern of protecting narrow leads in major tournaments rather than attempting to extend them.

England captain Harry Kane also admitted his side became too passive after taking the lead.

"When we went 1-0 up we seemed to try and hold on, which at this level is not enough," Kane said. "It was just wave after wave."

Tuchel defended his decisions after the match, insisting he had "no regrets" and explaining that England were already struggling to deal with Argentina's attacking pressure before the substitutions were made.

"We decided to go to a back five to close the gaps," Tuchel said. "The responsibility is on the coach. When it doesn't go well, it's easy to say it was wrong."

Despite guiding England to the semi-finals in his first major tournament, Tuchel now faces significant questions over whether his cautious tactical approach ultimately cost England a place in the World Cup final. The defeat leaves Argentina preparing to face Spain on Sunday, while England are left reflecting on another major tournament that ended with promise but concluded in disappointment.
