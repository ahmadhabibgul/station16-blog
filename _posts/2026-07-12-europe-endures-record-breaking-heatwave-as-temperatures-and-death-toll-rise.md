Europe is facing one of its most severe heatwaves in modern history, with record-breaking temperatures, widespread health emergencies, and mounting evidence linking the extreme weather event to climate change.

According to the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) and the European Union's Copernicus Climate Change Service, June 2026 was officially the hottest June ever recorded in Western Europe. The continent also experienced its second-warmest June globally, as persistent high-pressure systems trapped hot air over much of southern and western Europe for weeks.

### Temperatures Push Beyond 40°C

Countries including Spain, Portugal, France, Italy, and parts of Germany experienced temperatures exceeding **40°C**, prompting authorities to issue red heat alerts, close schools, and restrict outdoor activities.

The prolonged heat has placed significant pressure on healthcare systems and critical infrastructure. Rail services experienced delays due to overheating tracks, energy demand surged as millions relied on air conditioning, and wildfire risks increased dramatically across Mediterranean regions.

### More Than 10,000 Excess Deaths Recorded

Health experts estimate that the late-June heatwave contributed to **more than 10,000 excess deaths** across 27 European countries in a single week. Data from EuroMOMO, a mortality monitoring network supported by European public health agencies, indicates that the overwhelming majority of victims were adults aged 65 and older.

France and Belgium recorded some of the highest excess mortality rates during the period, while researchers in England and Wales estimate approximately 2,700 heat-related deaths occurred during May and June combined. Scientists note that many fatalities resulted from heat aggravating existing cardiovascular and respiratory conditions rather than direct heatstroke alone.

### Climate Scientists Draw Clear Connection

Researchers from the World Weather Attribution initiative concluded that the intensity of this year's heatwave would have been **"virtually impossible"** without human-induced climate change.

Human activity has increased global average temperatures by approximately 1.4°C since the pre-industrial era, significantly increasing both the likelihood and severity of extreme heat events. Scientists warn that such heatwaves, once considered rare, are becoming increasingly frequent across Europe.

### Daily Life Disrupted Across the Continent

The extreme temperatures have affected nearly every aspect of daily life.

In Germany, authorities reported an unusually high number of drowning fatalities as people sought relief in lakes and rivers during the heat. Across France, several tourist attractions temporarily closed or shortened operating hours, while some outdoor events were cancelled because of wildfire risks and dangerous temperatures. Parts of Spain also battled destructive wildfires that forced evacuations and damaged large areas of countryside.

### A Growing Challenge for Europe

Climate experts argue that Europe remains particularly vulnerable to rising temperatures because many cities were designed for historically milder summers. Older buildings often lack cooling systems, urban areas retain heat overnight, and aging populations face greater health risks during prolonged periods of extreme weather.

Meteorologists warn that further periods of intense heat remain possible throughout July, particularly across southern and eastern Europe, where temperatures may again exceed seasonal averages. Governments continue urging residents to stay hydrated, avoid strenuous outdoor activity during peak afternoon hours, and regularly check on elderly or vulnerable family members.

As Europe confronts another summer defined by extreme temperatures, the latest heatwave has reinforced warnings from scientists that adapting to a warming climate is becoming an increasingly urgent public health and economic priority.
