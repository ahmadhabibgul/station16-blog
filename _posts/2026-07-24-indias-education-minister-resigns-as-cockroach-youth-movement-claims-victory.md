India's Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned on Saturday, bowing to weeks of youth-led protests that demanded his ouster over a string of leaked entrance exam papers and broader failures in the country's testing system.

The unrest traces back to May, when the entrance exam for the country's most competitive medical schools was compromised, forcing officials to cancel results and order a retest that disrupted roughly two million students. The fallout fed into wider frustration over job scarcity and a lack of government accountability, giving rise to a youth movement calling itself the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP).

Demonstrators camped for over a month at New Delhi's Jantar Mantar, and the protests escalated sharply after police used batons, tear gas and reportedly pellet guns against crowds marching toward parliament, leaving more than 100 people injured. The scale of the movement made it one of the most serious domestic challenges Modi has faced in his third term — and a notable departure from his usual approach, given his history of resisting calls to remove embattled ministers.

In a message announcing his departure, Pradhan said he had submitted his resignation to Modi and voiced respect for the demands of the country's young people. As of Saturday, Modi had not publicly confirmed acceptance of the resignation.

Hours later, CJP organizers said the government had agreed to all of the movement's demands and called on protesters to disperse. Besides Pradhan's removal, those demands reportedly included compensation for the families of students who died by suicide after being forced to retake the exam. Authorities also restored mobile internet service around Jantar Mantar, which had been cut for most of the week.

Opposition Congress leader Rahul Gandhi welcomed the resignation but said police accountability for injuries sustained during the crackdown was still owed, and called on Modi to apologize for the force used against demonstrators.

A successor to Pradhan had not been named as of Saturday evening.
