Spain are world champions once again after defeating defending champions Argentina 1-0 in a tense 2026 FIFA World Cup final, securing their second World Cup title. However, the match will be remembered as much for the controversy surrounding Argentina’s performance and the scenes after the final whistle as for Spain’s triumph.

The decisive moment arrived in extra time when Ferran Torres scored in the 106th minute, heading in Nico Williams’ delivery to break the deadlock after a tightly contested encounter. Spain had controlled large portions of the match, while Argentina struggled to create meaningful attacking opportunities throughout regular time.

For Argentina, the defeat marked a dramatic fall from their status as reigning world champions. Lionel Scaloni’s side entered the tournament seeking back-to-back World Cup victories, but their final performance drew widespread criticism. Argentina failed to register a shot during regulation time and appeared increasingly frustrated as Spain dominated possession and territory.

The situation worsened late in the match when Enzo Fernández was sent off after receiving a second yellow card, leaving Argentina with ten men heading into extra time. The dismissal became another major talking point, with Argentine players and supporters questioning the decision, while others argued that discipline problems had contributed to their downfall.

Former players and pundits criticised Argentina’s approach, with many describing the team’s behaviour and lack of attacking ambition as unacceptable for a defending champion. The focus quickly shifted from their tactical struggles to their conduct during and after the match, creating a damaging ending to their title defence.

The controversy reached its peak after the final whistle when clashes broke out between players and staff from both teams. The confrontation began after Argentine midfielder Leandro Paredes became involved in an altercation with Spanish players, escalating into a larger incident involving multiple members of both squads. FIFA has since opened an investigation into the post-match scenes.

The disorder continued during Spain’s trophy celebrations, with reports of further confrontations and criticism directed at Argentina’s players for their reaction to defeat. The scenes created an uncomfortable contrast with Lionel Messi, who was praised by some observers for maintaining professionalism despite the disappointment of losing what could be his final World Cup appearance.

Despite the controversy, Spain’s victory represents a major achievement for a new generation of Spanish talent. Led by emerging stars and a possession-based attacking style, Spain completed a tournament that reinforced their return to the top of international football.

Argentina, meanwhile, are left facing difficult questions about the future. A team that delivered one of football’s greatest stories by winning the 2022 World Cup now faces criticism over their inability to match Spain’s intensity and the behaviour that overshadowed their final appearance.

The 2026 World Cup final will therefore be remembered as two very different stories: Spain’s celebration of a new golden generation, and Argentina’s painful collapse on football’s biggest stage.
